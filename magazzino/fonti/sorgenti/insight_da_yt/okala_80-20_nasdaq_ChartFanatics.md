## 3. Entry Techniques: Combining Levels with Market Structure [00:17:41]

Okala shares **three main entry patterns** that integrate price levels with price structure for low-risk, high-reward trades:

### 3.1 Fork Setup (Mean Reversion Long) [00:18:18]

The fork setup identifies a strong move down (e.g., 100-150 points) followed by a wick candle where the price fails to break the recent low.

- A **long wick and short candle body** signal sellers easing and buyers stepping in.
- Entry occurs on the next candle testing that low but not breaking it.
- Stop loss placed slightly below the low, typically 10 points.
- First take profit target is 15 points above the entry; partial scale-outs thereafter.
- Captures reversions after a capitulatory sell-off.

### 3.2 H Pattern Setup (Continuation Short) [00:21:30]

Okala’s favorite because he prefers short trades and dislikes uptrends:

- Occurs after strong down moves, with subsequent weak retracements going into the 80 level.
- The price forms a **lowercase "h" pattern**, indicating a short-term continuation of downtrend.
- Setup anticipates rollover rejection off 80 without blindly shorting the level.
- Mixed with the “cross-section” entry (below) for precision.
  
### 3.3 Cross-Section Entry (Precise Trigger) [00:23:50]

This is the actual trigger within the H or other patterns:

- Price tests **the intersection between two candles’ bodies or wicks** near 80 or 20 levels.
- Once price touches and rejects the cross-section point, entry is triggered.
- Stops remain at 10 points to maintain risk discipline.
- Offers a **reliable confirmation of market turning points** aligned with level confluences.

### 3.4 Repair Entry (Magnet Candle) [00:35:47]

An additional one, called the repair entry, identifies candles with **no wick on one side**, representing prices where no trades were filled (a ‘magnet’ for price to revert):

- After a strong move down, a candle with **no upper wick** signals trapped participants above.
- Price tends to revert back up to “repair” the unfilled orders.
- This setup overlaps well with the fork setup and can be used as a target or entry confirmation.
- Reverse logic applies for short entries when no bottom wick is present after a strong upward move.
