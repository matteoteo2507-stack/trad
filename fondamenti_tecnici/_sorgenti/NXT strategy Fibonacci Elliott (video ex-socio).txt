# Simplified Trading Strategy Using Fibonacci and Elliott Waves for Consistent Profitability

This video explains a **mechanical, time-efficient trading strategy** designed to overcome common beginner hurdles: complexity and the need for advanced skills. The presenter, with 7 years of market experience, shares a systematic approach combining Fibonacci retracements/extensions and Elliott Wave theory to trade primarily on trend direction with a clear risk-to-reward setup.

## 1. Key Challenges in Trading Addressed by the Strategy

The strategy is developed to solve two main problems traders face:

- Trading is often perceived as too complicated and time-consuming, requiring constant chart monitoring.
- Trading demands extensive knowledge of complex market terms and techniques, discouraging many beginners.

**This system aims to be both simple and systematic**, using clearly defined steps and mechanical rules that reduce discretionary errors and time spent watching screens.

## 2. Core Tools and Concepts of the Strategy

The approach revolves around three main technical tools integrated into a strategic workflow:

- **Fibonacci Retracement:** Used to identify key price reaction zones between $0.5$ and $0.66$ levels (with important sub-levels at $0.382$, $0.5$, $0.618$, and $0.786$).
- **Fibonacci Extension:** Used to project probable price targets (point C) beyond the retracement, mainly focusing on extension levels like $1.618$, $1.89$, and $2.0$.
- **Elliott Waves:** Applied as a conceptual framework where the market moves in waves of expansion, retracement, and further expansion, helping to visualize price sequences and trend direction without complex wave counting.

**The market's movement in waves allows the trader to identify specific "checkpoints" for entries and exits.**

## 3. Understanding Market Movement and Trends

- The market moves in cycles of expansions and retracements.
- Trends are identified by observing **higher highs and higher lows in uptrends** or **lower highs and lower lows in downtrends**.
- Trading aligns with the prevailing trend to avoid difficulties associated with counter-trend trades.
- Important price levels are treated as psychological zones controlled by trader behavior.

## 4. Step-by-Step Commandments of the Strategy

Seven systematic steps form the checklist for trade setups, plus one bonus step related to trade management:

| Step | Action                                                                                   |
|-------|----------------------------------------------------------------------------------------|
| 1     | Identify a significant starting point: a major swing low for long trades or high for shorts (point $0$). |
| 2     | Draw Fibonacci retracement from point $0$ to the point where the retracement ends (point B). Set key levels between $0.5$ and $0.66$. |
| 3     | Wait for breakout of point A (retracement level) to confirm bullish/bearish continuation. Break of point A signals trade eligibility. |
| 4     | Use Fibonacci extension from points $0$, A, and B to identify potential target zone (point C) around key levels $1.618$, $1.89$, $2.0$. |
| 5     | Upon breakout confirmation, draw a new retracement from point B to the next retracement (forming BC zone). |
| 6     | Plan trade entry around the $0.5$ retracement within BC zone. Set stop-loss below $0.786$ retracement level to minimize losses. |
| 7     | Target a risk-reward ratio of **1:3** to maximize profitability and allow room for multiple trades even with some losses. |
| 8 (Bonus) | Move stop-loss to break-even once price reaches 1:2 reward level to protect capital and avoid drawdowns. |

**The strategy emphasizes mechanical rules and statistical probabilities without reliance on guesswork.**

## 5. Trading Timeframes and Execution Practicalities

- Strategy can be applied to any timeframe: weekly, daily, 4-hour, hourly, or even 1-minute charts depending on trader availability and preferred trade speed.
- Longer timeframes require less monitoring but longer waits; shorter timeframes are more active but require closer attention.
- Typically, the presenter uses the **hourly (H1) timeframe** for examples due to balance between frequency and clarity.

## 6. Real Chart Examples and Observations

- Start with a daily or higher timeframe to find a major pivot (swing high or low).
- Drop down to H1 or M30 for precise retracement identification.
- Identify valid retracement areas (point B) using Fibonacci levels.
- Confirm trade when price breaks point A.
- Define target with Fibonacci extension (point C).
- Draw subsequent retracements for new BC zones to place trades following the checklist.
- Monitor weekly market closes and avoid trading on Fridays due to the risk of unpredictable Monday openings.
- Acknowledge that not all sequences will develop or lead to valid entries; only proceed when the checklist criteria are fully met.

## 7. Advantages and Risk Management of the Strategy

- **Mechanical and low time commitment:** Trades take time to develop, so daily monitoring suffices.
- **Risk Reward Ratio:** 1:3 setup allows recovery from multiple losing trades.
- **High win rate potential:** Around **60-70% profitable trades out of 10**, based on adherence to rules.
- **Drawdown control:** Use of trailing stop loss to break-even after partial profit improves capital preservation.
- **Flexibility:** Applicable across multiple markets and adaptable timeframes.
- **No dependency on signals or external groups:** Entire process is self-managed based on objective technical criteria.

## 8. Limitations and Considerations

- Requires **consistent practice and discipline** to apply the full checklist.
- Not every price movement will produce a valid sequence or trade opportunity.
- Losses are expected but controlled by risk management.
- Avoid trading near week's end; market gaps can invalidate positions.
- Statistical edge means some trades fail; consistent application is key.
